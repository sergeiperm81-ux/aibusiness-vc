# Before You Sign, Ask the AI: How to Check a Company, a Person or a Website in Five Minutes, and Why Your Own Site Has to Pass the Same Test

You get an email from a supplier you have never heard of. Good price, decent website, a name in the signature. What do you do before you answer? Ten years ago you opened Google and typed the company name plus "reviews". Today a growing share of people open ChatGPT instead and type: "Is this company legit? Any red flags?" They get a paragraph back in ten seconds, with sources, and they decide.

Here is the part most business owners have not caught up with: the same thing is happening to you. Your prospect, your next hire, the investor you emailed last week, the journalist who might quote you. Before they reply, they ask an AI assistant who you are. What it says is now the first impression, and you have never seen it.

This is our own article about our own tools, and I will say that plainly rather than pretend it is neutral. We built three small checks for exactly this moment: [AI Company Scan](/company-scan), [AI Person Scan](/professional-scan) and [AI Website Visibility](/audit). This piece explains what they do, what they cost, when they are worth it, and what they cannot do. If you finish it and decide you can do the same thing by hand, that is a fine outcome too.

The point holds either way. The assistant's answer is now a step in the buying process, and it is a step that runs without you in the room.

## Why checking with AI beats not checking at all

Most small and mid-sized companies have no security department. They have a founder, a few people, and a rule of thumb: if the website looks fine and the email is polite, go ahead. That rule is what the $2.77 billion in the FBI's table is built on. Vendor impersonation works because checking is a chore, so people skip it.

An AI assistant with web search turns the chore into a ninety-second habit. Ask it "who is behind acme-supplies.com, what do they sell, and are there complaints, lawsuits or scam warnings?" and it reads the company's site, the registries and review platforms it can reach, the news, the forums, and comes back with a summary and links. It is not an investigator. It will miss things a paid due diligence firm finds, and it will sometimes confuse two companies with the same name. But the red flags that matter most for a small business, a trail of unpaid contractors, a regulator's warning, a domain registered last month, a founder with a previous company that folded owing money, show up in exactly the places these assistants read.

The trade is simple: a formal vendor due diligence report costs hundreds to thousands of dollars and takes days; a security team costs a salary. A five-model AI check costs less than lunch and takes five minutes, and for the deals under €50,000 that make up most of a small company's year, it is the difference between checking and not checking.

## Why one SEO ranking no longer gets you into the answer

Google and AI crawlers read sites differently, and this catches good sites all the time. A robots.txt rule written years ago can let Googlebot in and turn OAI-SearchBot and Claude-SearchBot away. Text that loads through JavaScript reaches a browser and Google, but most AI crawlers do not run scripts, so to them the page is empty. And an assistant with search does not rank pages, it picks a handful to read and quotes them, which is why a competitor's blog post about you can become "the source" while your own site is skipped. I wrote about the mechanics, and why so many experts are missing from the answer to "who can help with AI governance", in [Your Name Is Not in the Answer](/notes/your-name-is-not-in-the-answer).

None of this is a promise that fixing the site gets you recommended. Nobody can promise that, and anyone selling it is selling something else. Readability is the first step, not the last. But a site the assistants cannot read is out of the answer by definition, and that is the part you can fix this week.

A €14.97 check that surfaces one regulator's warning or a founder's previous collapse pays for itself roughly 1,300 times over on the first save. You will run many checks that find nothing. ## What this means for you

**If you buy from small suppliers.** Make the Company Scan part of the onboarding form. Website address in, report by email in about five minutes, one line in your notes: "checked, no flags" or "checked, one dispute, asked about it." It is the same habit as asking for a VAT number, and it catches what a VAT number does not.

**If you sell your own expertise.** Run the Person Scan on yourself before your next outreach campaign, not after. If a model finds nothing, or finds someone else, that is what your prospects are being told, and the fix is public pages that put your name next to your role, your company and your city. The report's recommendations are specific about which pages. Then run it on the three people you are about to negotiate with.

**If you run a company site.** Run the free visibility check now, it takes a minute, and hand the result to whoever maintains the site. Most fixes are a developer's afternoon: a robots.txt line, server-rendered text, an Organization schema block. If you are building something new, check the landing page before you spend on traffic, because a launch nobody's assistant can read is a launch at half volume. And if you are one of the [agencies and freelancers who sell to small businesses](/solo/ai-automation-agency-guide), the free check is the best cold opener we know: "ChatGPT cannot read your homepage, here is why" gets more replies than "we do AI automation."

**If you are choosing a market.** Before you commit to a vertical, [run the incumbents through the Company Scan](/startups/ai-startup-ideas-2026). Where the models surface complaints and warnings about the established players, a market's reputation is soft, and that is where a careful newcomer wins.

## What these checks cannot do

We would rather you know the limits than find them in a report.

The models say what they say. We record it with its sources; we do not verify it and we do not claim it is true. Ask twice and the wording changes, sometimes the facts. Every report is a snapshot with a date on it. The answers come from each provider's API with search on, not from the consumer apps, which add their own instructions and remember their users, so your own ChatGPT may say something slightly different.

The Person Scan is not a background check, and we are not a consumer reporting agency: do not use it for hiring, tenancy, credit or insurance decisions. It shows what AI says about a person, which can be wrong or about someone else, and the report says so on its cover. The Company Scan checks the organisation behind a website, not its legal ownership or registration; for a registry extract you still need the registry. And the visibility check reads the homepage, robots.txt and llms.txt on a live request, the way a crawler does; it does not crawl deep pages, and it does not measure whether you will appear in anyone's answer.
