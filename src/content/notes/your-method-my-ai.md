You have a method. I have AI. What happens if we combine them?

Yesterday, a franchise expert who helps companies scale reached out to me.

He has clients, experience, and a proven methodology. But he is tired of repeating the same manual steps.

For example, competitor analysis.

He already uses AI, but still has to:

- write prompts;
- enter company and competitor data;
- review and clean the results;
- assemble and format the final report.

So he asked: can I keep the meaningful expert work and hand the repetitive routine over to AI?

I said: let's do it.

I will build a dedicated platform where key stages of his methodology can be launched with one click, powered by a series of configured AI agents.

For competitor analysis, he will only need to enter the company's website.

The AI will then:

- identify direct and indirect competitors;
- collect data and sources;
- compare the companies;
- conduct the analysis;
- prepare a professionally formatted report.

The expert will review the result, add his insights, and make the strategic decisions.

I will also create a client portal where clients can view results, receive assignments, and submit information for AI to process.

AI does not replace a strong expert. It turns their methodology into a scalable system.

From separate methods into one manageable process. From repetitive work into a product. From eight hours of expert time into the ability to manage more projects without reducing quality.
