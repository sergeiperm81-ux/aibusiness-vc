AI capability is not AI authority. Most companies acquired the first without ever defining the second.

That is the argument of a paper I have just published together with Amanda Cunningham, founder of Savvy Pixel.

Her half covers what happens inside the organisation. AI never formally joins a company. Nobody interviews it, nobody signs anything. It arrives through accounts, features and updates, and one day it is talking to your customers. Her fix: induct AI the way you would induct a new employee, with eight questions leadership must be able to answer. Purpose, context, access, authority, boundaries, accountability, evidence, review.

My half starts where hers ends: the moment the AI meets a customer. From 2 August 2026, Article 50 of the EU AI Act requires telling people they are talking to an AI. That is the legal floor, not the customer proposition. A customer has three real questions: how does this company use AI at all, what can this particular bot do, and how do I prove my case when something goes wrong. Three documents answer them: an AI policy, an AI service passport, an AI receipt. And a periodic test purchase checks that the answers are still true.

Induction establishes the promise inside the organisation. Accountability tests whether that promise survives contact with the real world.
